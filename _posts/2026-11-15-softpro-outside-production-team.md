SoftPro is one of the most widely used closing and title production systems in the United States, and many of the offices that run on it reach the same point sooner or later: the work is there, the software is fine, and there are not enough hands to type it all.

Bringing in an outside production team is a common answer. This guide covers how to set one up in SoftPro so that the work is done your way, inside your system, with your staff in control of the result.

## Why do the work inside SoftPro

There are two ways an outside team can work on your files. One is to send documents out and receive finished ones back. The other is to give the team access to your SoftPro system and have them work there.

Working inside your system is the better arrangement for most offices:

- **Nothing leaves.** Files and documents stay in SoftPro. Nothing has to be downloaded, emailed around or stored elsewhere.
- **Your templates are used.** Commitments and policies come out in your documents and your wording.
- **The file is always current.** Your staff see the work in the order as soon as it is done, without importing anything.
- **You control access.** You decide what the team can open and change, and you can remove access at any time.

## Step 1: Decide which tasks to hand over

Start narrow. The tasks that transfer most easily are the ones where everything needed is already in the order:

- **Order entry:** opening new orders and keying parties, property and figures.
- **Commitment typing and examination:** preparing the commitment from your search package.
- **Final policies:** preparing owner's and loan policies after closing.

Pick one, run it for a few weeks, then add the next.

## Step 2: Set up access

Create named users for the outside team, with only the permissions the chosen tasks need. Talk to your SoftPro administrator, and to SoftPro support if you are unsure how your edition handles user security and remote access. Check your own underwriter and vendor-management requirements at the same stage.

A few sensible rules:

- one login per person, never shared;
- no access to escrow accounting or disbursement unless there is a clear reason;
- a named contact on each side for questions.

## Step 3: Show them how you work

Every SoftPro office is set up differently. Your document templates, your standard exceptions, your order numbering and the way you use notes and tasks are all specific to you.

There are two good ways to pass this on. Either walk the team through a file in your system, or give them reference commitments and policies you have already issued, so they can learn your wording and how you present each detail: deeds, mortgages, liens, chain of title, taxes and so on. Doing both is better still.

## Step 4: Get it in writing

The outside team should turn what they learned into a written standard operating procedure and send it to you for approval. It should cover, at least:

- how orders are numbered and named;
- which templates are used for which transaction types;
- your standard requirement and exception wording, and the order you list them in;
- how recording information and policy dates are entered;
- what to do when information is missing;
- who to ask.

Do not let work begin until you have approved it. Fixing a misunderstanding in a document takes minutes. Fixing it across fifty files takes days.

## Step 5: Build in the review

The team prepares. Your staff review and issue. Decide in advance who on your side re-checks commitments and policies, and what they look at: names against the vesting deed, the legal description, requirements against the examination, exceptions against the search.

A good outside team will run its own quality check before the work reaches you. That does not replace yours.

## Step 6: Start with a trial

Begin with a small number of live files and review every one closely. Give specific feedback, have the SOP updated, and only then increase the volume.

## A note on SoftPro 360 and integrations

SoftPro continues to add integrations that affect how production work flows. SoftPro describes [SoftPro 360](https://blog.softprocorp.com/i-didnt-know-softpro-could-do-that-5-ways-to-use-softpro-360) as a way to connect to third-party title and escrow services without leaving SoftPro, and in October 2026 it [announced an integration with Hopdox](https://blog.softprocorp.com/introducing-hopdox-integration-in-softpro-360) for e-recording documents from within the software.

For an office working with an outside team, the point is the same as everywhere else in this guide: the more that happens inside the order, the less has to be passed around, and the easier it is for your staff to see and check what was done. When you write your SOP, note which of these services your office uses and who is responsible for each step.
