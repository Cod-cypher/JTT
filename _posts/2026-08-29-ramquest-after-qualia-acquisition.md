RamQuest has been one of the most widely used title production systems in the country for decades. In January 2025 it changed hands, and many of the title agents who run on it are still working out what that means for them.

This article sets out what has been said publicly, the questions worth putting to your vendor, and how to keep files moving if a software change is in your future.

## What happened

On January 29, 2025, Old Republic Title and Qualia announced a strategic technology partnership. As part of it, [Qualia's press release](https://www.qualia.com/press-releases/old-republic-title-and-qualia-announce-strategic-technology-partnership/) said Qualia would acquire Old Republic Title's RamQuest and E-Closing platforms.

[HousingWire reported](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/qualia-acquires-ramquest-e-closing-from-old-republic/) the same day that the financial terms were not disclosed, and described RamQuest as one of the platforms most widely used by title professionals, alongside Qualia and SoftPro. According to that report, Qualia's chief executive said Old Republic would move the majority of its direct title operation onto the Qualia platform as part of the partnership.

## What has been said about RamQuest customers

Public statements have focused on continuity. In March 2025, [Qualia's write-up of the RamQuest User Group Conference](https://blog.qualia.com/five-takeaways-rqug-conference/) said that current RamQuest customers keep receiving support on their existing platform until they move to Qualia, with regular updates along the way, and that customers were encouraged to ask questions, evaluate and choose their own timing. It also referred to a transition timeline agreed between the two companies.

What we have not found in the public announcements is a single, official statement of exact end dates for each RamQuest product. Third-party articles quote specific support periods, but we have not been able to confirm those against Qualia or RamQuest directly, so we are not repeating them here.

**The practical step is simple: ask.** Your RamQuest support contact can tell you what applies to your product and your contract. Get the answer in writing.

## Questions to ask your vendor

- How long will my current RamQuest product be supported, and where is that stated?
- Will it continue to receive updates, including underwriter form and rate changes?
- What migration paths are offered, and what do they cost?
- What happens to my historical files and documents after a move?
- Which of my current integrations carry over?
- How long does a typical migration take for an office my size?
- Who trains my staff, and when?

## If you stay on RamQuest for now

Nothing forces a rushed decision. If RamQuest is working for your office and remains supported, staying put while you evaluate is reasonable. Use the time well:

- **Document how you work.** Record your templates, exception wording, file naming and workflows. You will need this for any future system, and it is valuable even if you never move.
- **Clean up open work.** Clear outstanding final policies and close out old files. A migration is far easier with a tidy system.
- **Know your data.** Find out what you can export and in what form.

## If you are planning a migration

A software migration is one of the hardest things a title office does, because the files do not stop coming while it happens. A few things make it go better:

**Pick your timing.** Avoid your busiest months if you can.

**Expect a dip.** Staff are slower in a new system for the first weeks. Plan for it instead of being surprised by it.

**Do not migrate a backlog.** Every unfinished task you carry across becomes harder to finish. Clear what you can first.

**Protect production during training.** The hours your team spends in training and setup are hours not spent typing commitments and preparing policies. This is where an outside production team helps most: someone keeps the work moving in your current system while your people learn the new one.

**Rewrite your procedures for the new system.** Templates and workflows rarely transfer exactly. Budget time to rebuild and re-approve them.
