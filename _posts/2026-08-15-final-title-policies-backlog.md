The closing is done, the parties have their keys and their funds, and the file feels finished. It is not. The final title policy still has to be prepared and issued, and in a busy office that is exactly the task that slips.

Final policies fall behind in most title companies at some point. This guide explains why, what a backlog costs, and how to clear one without letting new files pile up behind it.

## What a final title policy is

The final policy is the title insurance policy itself, issued after closing. It replaces the commitment. Where the commitment said what would be insured if certain conditions were met, the policy says what is insured now that they have been.

Most transactions produce one or two policies: an owner's policy for the buyer and a loan policy for the lender.

## What goes into a final policy

Preparing a policy means updating the commitment to reflect what actually happened at closing:

- **Policy date**, taken from the recording of the insured documents.
- **Insured names and policy amounts**, confirmed against the closed file.
- **Vesting**, now in the buyer's name, as the recorded deed shows it.
- **Recording details** for the deed and the mortgage: book and page or instrument number, and the recording date.
- **Requirements**: those satisfied at closing drop away.
- **Exceptions**: those that still apply carry into Schedule B, in your wording.
- **Endorsements**: added as the commitment and the lender's instructions require.

Each of these has to match the closed file and the recorded documents. That is why a policy cannot be prepared until the recorded documents are back.

## Why final policies fall behind

**Nothing is waiting on them.** Before closing, every task has someone asking for it. After closing, nobody calls to ask where the policy is, at least not for a while.

**They depend on recording.** The policy needs recording information, and recorded documents can take days or weeks to return. By then the file is out of mind.

**New orders always feel more urgent.** The same people who prepare policies usually also work pre-closing files. When volume rises, the pre-closing work wins.

**They take concentration.** A policy is not hard, but it requires opening the closed file, the recorded documents and the commitment and reconciling them. It is easy to put off.

## What a backlog costs

- **Lenders notice.** Lenders expect their loan policies within a set time after closing, and late policies affect how they see your office.
- **Underwriters notice.** Policy reporting and remittance run on your underwriter's schedule.
- **Details fade.** A policy prepared months later takes longer, because nobody remembers the file's quirks.
- **It grows.** A backlog that is not actively worked gets bigger every week.

## How to clear a final policy backlog

### 1. Count it

List every closed file without an issued policy, with the closing date. You cannot plan around a number you do not know.

### 2. Sort it

Split the list into files that are ready, meaning recorded documents are in, and files that are waiting on something. Work the ready ones. Chase the others separately.

### 3. Work oldest first

Lenders and underwriters care most about the oldest files, and they only get harder with time.

### 4. Set a daily number

A backlog is cleared by a steady daily count, not by a heroic weekend. Decide how many backlog policies get prepared each day in addition to current ones.

### 5. Keep current files current

This is where most catch-up efforts fail. If new closings are added to the pile while the old ones are cleared, the backlog never ends. Current policies should be prepared as soon as recording comes back, alongside the backlog.

### 6. Add capacity for the catch-up

Clearing a backlog is extra work on top of normal work. It usually needs extra hands for a period, whether that is overtime, a temporary hire or an outside production team.

## Staying current afterwards

Once the backlog is gone, three habits keep it from returning:

- **Trigger the policy from recording.** When recorded documents come in, the policy task starts the same day.
- **Give policies an owner.** One person, or one team, is responsible for the count.
- **Watch one number.** Closed files without a policy, checked weekly. If it rises, act early.
