Title volume does not arrive evenly. A slow month is followed by a rush, and the production work, the typing, keying and preparing, is what backs up first. Hiring for the peak leaves you overstaffed in the trough. Not hiring leaves your best people typing at night.

Outsourcing title production is one answer, and it works well when it is set up carefully. This guide covers which tasks to hand off first, what should stay with your team, how to vet a provider and how to start without risk to live files.

## What title production outsourcing is

It means having an outside team do defined back-office tasks on your files: opening orders, preparing search packages, examining and typing commitments, preparing final policies. The outside team prepares. Your team reviews, decides and issues.

It is not handing over your closings, your client relationships or your underwriting judgment.

## What to hand off first

The best first tasks share three traits: the inputs are already in the file, the steps can be written down, and the result is quick for your staff to check.

### 1. Final policies

Usually the easiest place to start. Everything needed is in the closed file, the work is the same from file to file, and nothing is waiting on it at a closing table. If you have a backlog, it is also where you will feel the difference soonest.

### 2. Order entry

Well defined and easy to verify. A file is either opened correctly or it is not, and a quick comparison against the contract tells you which.

### 3. Commitment typing and examination

This is higher skill and higher stakes, so it deserves a written procedure and a firm review step. Once those are in place it is where the most staff hours are saved, because typing commitments is the longest production task in most offices.

### 4. Search preparation

Gathering and organizing what your searcher needs, so the search itself starts with everything in place.

Many companies begin with one task, watch the results for a few weeks, then add the next.

## What to keep in-house

- **Underwriting decisions and final review.** The company that issues the commitment or policy has to stand behind it.
- **Issuing.** Commitments and policies go out under your name and your agency.
- **Closings and funds.** Escrow, disbursement and the closing table are yours.
- **Client relationships.** Agents, lenders and buyers should deal with your people.

A good provider will tell you plainly what they do not do. Be cautious of one who claims to do everything.

## How to vet a title production provider

**Where does the work happen?** The safest arrangement is one where the provider works inside your own software with access you control, so that files are not downloaded, copied out or stored elsewhere. Ask directly.

**Who touches the files?** You should know whether the work is done by the provider's own team or passed on to others.

**Will they follow your procedure?** Every title company has its own templates, exception wording and naming rules. The provider should learn yours and write them down for you to approve, not ask you to adapt to theirs.

**How is quality checked?** Ask what review happens before work comes back to you, and expect to re-check it yourself as well.

**What is the turnaround, and when do they work?** A team in another time zone can be an advantage: files sent in the evening can be back by morning.

**Can you try them first?** A short trial on real files tells you more than any sales call.

**How is it priced?** Per-file pricing is the simplest to budget, because your cost moves with your volume.

**Are they honest about what they are?** Onshore or offshore, large or small, a provider should say so plainly. So should you, if your underwriter or clients ask who works on your files. Check your own underwriter and vendor-management requirements before you start.

## How to start safely

1. **Show them how you work.** Either walk the provider through a file in your software, or send reference commitments and policies so they can learn your wording and how you present each detail: deeds, mortgages, liens, chain of title, taxes and the rest.
2. **Get the procedure in writing.** The provider should turn what they learned into a standard operating procedure and send it to you.
3. **Approve it before any work begins.** Corrections are cheap at this stage and expensive later.
4. **Run a small trial.** Start with a handful of files and review every one closely.
5. **Scale up gradually**, one task and one volume step at a time.

## What it does to your day

When it is working, the change is simple. Files arrive typed and checked. Your examiners and processors spend their time reviewing instead of keying. Your closers get back the hours they were spending on production. The goal is not to replace your team. It is to let them spend the day on closings and on the clients who send you business.
